On October 1st, Lena and Marcus had $4,812.37 across checking and savings. Neither paycheck had landed yet, and rent was due on the 3rd. This is how they planned it with only the money they already had.

## Start with the money you have

A zero based budget never counts income before it arrives. Every dollar in a budget account goes into Ready to Assign and waits there until you give it a job. Their October started with $4,812.37 waiting.

## Give every dollar a job

They worked from the most urgent costs down, and stopped only when Ready to Assign read $0.00:

1. **Bills first.** Rent $1,450.00, utilities $102.00 and the phone plan $65.00.
2. **Then the next 30 days.** Groceries $612.40 and transportation $170.00.
3. **Then what is coming.** $142.38 for January's car insurance and $250.00 for emergencies.
4. **Then the goals.** $380.00 toward flights to Lisbon, and the remaining $1,640.59 to savings.

When the paychecks arrived on the 15th, they went to Ready to Assign too, and got their jobs the same way: next month's rent first, then the rest.

## When you overspend, move money

On the 18th, groceries reached $648.10, which is $35.70 over plan. Instead of letting it spill into November, they moved $35.70 from Dining out. The plan changed, and it still added up.

<PullQuote>
  A plan that changes is still a plan.<br />A plan you stop opening is not.
</PullQuote>

## Roll it forward

On November 1st, whatever was left in each category rolled forward. Groceries started again from $0.00, the emergency fund held $250.00, and the flights fund had $380.00 of its $1,200.00 target.

Nothing about the month was clever. Every dollar had a job before it was spent, and when a job changed, another one gave way.
