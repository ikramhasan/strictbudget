Money conversations go wrong when two people are looking at different numbers. A shared budget fixes the numbers. The rest is a few roles and a few habits.

## Who can do what

A budget has one owner and up to five more people. Each of them is either an **editor**, who can enter transactions and move money, or a **viewer**, who can see everything and change nothing. A viewer suits a teenager learning how the household runs, or an accountant at tax time.

Changes appear for everyone as they happen. When one person assigns money, the other sees Ready to Assign drop without refreshing.

## The history settles it

Every change is in the budget's history, with who made it and when. "Who moved $40.00 out of Dining out?" has an answer that takes five seconds to find, which tends to end the conversation before it starts.

<Tip title="Invite by email or by link">
  Invitations go out by email. On a self hosted server without email, you share the invitation link yourself.
</Tip>

## A few house rules

1. **Agree on the big categories once.** Rent, groceries and savings targets get set together; nobody moves them alone.
2. **Give each person a category of their own.** A personal spending category, funded the same for both, removes most of the small arguments.
3. **Fix overspending the same day.** Whoever overspends moves the money to cover it, so the other person never finds a red number.
4. **Look at the plan together once a month.** Ten minutes on the first is enough.
