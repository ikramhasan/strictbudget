A credit card feels like spending later. In a zero based budget it is spending now, with money you already have. The card's payment category makes sure that money is still there when the bill arrives.

## Every card has a payment category

When you add a credit card account, strictbudget creates a category for paying it. You don't assign to it like other categories. It fills itself as you spend.

## What happens when you buy something

Say Groceries has $612.40 and you pay $86.17 at the supermarket with your Visa. Groceries drops by $86.17, and the Visa payment category grows by $86.17. The money moved from "food this month" to "the card bill", so the purchase is already paid for in your plan.

When the statement comes, you pay the card from checking, and the payment category goes back down. Nothing new had to be found.

## When a category didn't have enough

If Groceries only had $50.00 and you spent $86.17 on the card, the $36.17 that wasn't covered can't move to the payment category. The budget shows it as credit overspending, in yellow, and the payment category won't cover the full balance. Move money into Groceries to fix it, and the payment category catches up.

<Tip title="Carrying a balance">
  For debt you already had, assign money to the payment category directly. Its target can plan a monthly payment until the card is clear.
</Tip>

## The habit

Treat the card like a debit card that pays you back in points. If the category has it, spend it. If not, move money first.
