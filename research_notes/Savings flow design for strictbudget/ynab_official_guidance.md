# YNAB's official position, product mechanics and messaging on savings (state as of 2026-11-12, with history)

Method note for the report writer. Everything under "Cited Findings" was read from the live source on 2026-11-12 unless
labelled otherwise. YNAB help articles were fetched as raw markdown (`<article-url>.md`) so quotes are exact; each help
article carries a "Last updated" stamp, which I give. The Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) and archive.ph were
unreachable from this environment, so older help-doc wording could NOT be retrieved first-hand; where I rely on a
third-party copy of an old YNAB post, or on a search-result summary, it is labelled. Several older YNAB blog posts now
301-redirect to newer pages (noted where relevant), which is itself evidence of guidance being retired.

## 1. Mechanics: on-budget ("Cash") vs tracking accounts, and what transfers do to Ready to Assign and categories

### Takeaway
YNAB today has four account groups (Cash, Credit, Loan, Tracking). Only Cash/Credit money is "in the plan"; Tracking
and Loan balances are not assignable. A Cash→Cash transfer (checking→savings) needs no category and changes nothing in
the plan; any transfer between Cash and a Tracking or Loan account requires a category because "money is either
entering or leaving your plan". The account type cannot be changed after creation.

### Cited Findings
- Account type definitions (help article last updated 2026-08-11): "**Cash Accounts** are those from which you will spend money in the near future (usually within the next year or two)." / "**Credit Accounts** are those that generally have a negative balance, such as credit card and line of credit accounts." / "**Loan Accounts** help you track your loans and plan for your loan payments." / "**Tracking Accounts** hold money you don't plan to spend soon, such as investments." — [Account Types (Cash, Credit, Loan, and Tracking)](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq)
- The same article's meta description still reads "YNAB has three types of accounts: Budget Loan or Tracking", while the body says "Understand YNAB's four account types: cash, credit, loan, and tracking." (stale wording from the earlier "Budget accounts" naming) — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq.md)
- Cash account sub-types offered at creation: "Checking (typically used for everyday spending)", "Savings (It's important to assign your savings, too!)", "Cash (transactions in this type of account are automatically marked as cleared…)". A "Savings" account is therefore just a Cash account with a different label; the article describes no behavioural difference. — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq)
- Cash accounts "typically have a positive balance and should match the cash in your categories and Ready to Assign." — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq)
- Tracking accounts: "The dollars in tracking accounts are not included in your plan. Adding these accounts to YNAB allows you to **track** their balance". "Since the balances in tracking accounts don't impact your plan, you don't need to add any of them to YNAB. But you can if you want to." Two options: "**Asset**: investment accounts." and "**Liability**: other loans, such as money owed between family or friends…" — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq)
- "The money in your **loan** accounts and **tracking** accounts are not included in your plan (since you're just tracking those accounts' balances). These funds won't be available to assign to your categories." — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq)
- Tracking accounts and linking: "Tracking accounts can be linked, depending on the type of account, though manually updating them tends to be more reliable… it's common for them to import incorrectly or not be able to connect at all. Additionally, we aren't able to troubleshoot connection issues with tracking accounts." — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq)
- Transfer category rule, exact wording (article last updated 2026-09-24): "**No category needed** if you're transferring between two accounts of the same type. **Cash → Cash** (e.g., checking to savings): the money stays in your plan. **Tracking → Tracking**: the money is outside your plan either way." / "**Category required** if you're transferring between a Cash account and a **Tracking or Loan** account—because money is either entering or leaving your plan." / "A common example: if your savings account is set up as a Tracking account in YNAB, a transfer to your checking account will require a category." — [Transfer Transactions](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/transfer-transactions-a-guide-HJOsZz4Jj)
- Web UI wording: "In the Category field, YNAB will most likely show "Category not needed." You'll only need to choose a category if you're making a transfer between a Cash account and a **Tracking or Loan** account" — [Transfer Transactions](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/transfer-transactions-a-guide-HJOsZz4Jj)
- Cash→Cash (checking→savings) effect on the plan (article last updated 2026-07-10): "making a transfer from a checking account to a savings account (one Cash account to another) doesn't require a category or change anything in your plan. That's because **you already have a plan for those dollars.** In YNAB, categories are used to organize your spending and saving dollars, like in a cash envelope system. Bank accounts aren't." and "If you'd like to increase a savings category by the amount of the transfer, move money from _Ready to Assign_ or another category." — [How to Assign Your Savings](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-assign-your-savings-rkJ_Yy3Rq)
- If only one side of a real-world transfer exists in YNAB, it is not a transfer at all: "If instead the **sending** account is the only one set up in YNAB, this is a spending transaction, not a transfer in YNAB." / "If instead the **receiving** account is the only one set up in YNAB, it's an income transaction". — [Transfer Transactions](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/transfer-transactions-a-guide-HJOsZz4Jj)
- Cash→Tracking transfers are treated as expenses in reports: "Income v Expense includes transfers between **Cash/Credit** accounts and **Tracking** accounts (which require a category). Even though you're moving money to another account (which doesn't feel quite the same as spending), the funds are leaving your plan." and "Assigning money to a savings category isn't the same as spending it, so that money won't show up as an expense in Income v Expense until it's actually spent from that category. Only transfers into a Tracking account (not a regular savings category in a Cash account) count as an expense in this report." — [Income vs. Spending and Income v Expense](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/income-v-expense-Byu1BYWRq)
- Tracking-account activity is excluded from spending/income reports: "Activity in cash accounts will appear when you reflect with Spending Trends, Spending Breakdown, Income v Expense, and Net Worth, while loan and tracking account activity appears only in Net Worth." — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq)
- Account type is immutable: "No. It's not possible to change the account type once you've added it to YNAB. If you need a different account type, you can replace it instead." — [Can I change the type of account…](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/can-i-change-the-type-of-account-i-m-using-ryENm_m7Jx). The replacement guide warns: "Changing your account will remove money from your plan. This may appear as a lower or negative Ready to Assign." — [Changing an Account Type](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/changing-an-account-type-a-guide-HJRnSXWko)
- Nothing links a category to an account (except credit card payment and loan pairings): "there is nothing in the plan that links an account balance to a single category or group of categories." — [How to Add a Health Savings Account in YNAB](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-add-a-health-savings-account-in-ynab-ByRXzZOAc). Matching a savings account balance to a category group is a manual convention the help centre illustrates ("The Savings account balance matches the total amount available in the Wants category group") and simultaneously discourages: "while it is possible to try to match category balances to account balances, it can lead to confusion and unnecessary duplication of effort." — [How to Assign Your Savings](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-assign-your-savings-rkJ_Yy3Rq); [Category Balances versus Account Balances](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/category-balances-versus-account-balances-an-overview-ryvnKB_Ac)
- API-level confirmation of the two-way split: "an account is either "on budget", where its activity is categorized and planned, or a tracking account, where only its balance is tracked (see the on_budget flag)." and "Splits are not allowed on tracking accounts or on transfers between on-budget accounts; a transfer to a tracking account can be a split." — [YNAB OpenAPI spec v1.91.0](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)

### Inferences
- Summary table derived from the above: (a) Cash→Cash: no category, Ready to Assign (RTA) unchanged, category balances unchanged. (b) Cash→Tracking: category required; the outflow reduces that category's Available exactly like spending and is reported as an expense; RTA is unaffected provided the category was funded. (c) Tracking→Cash: category required; money re-enters the plan as a categorized inflow. YNAB's docs do not say in one place which category to use for (c); by analogy with their income guidance ("Categorize it as Income: Ready to Assign (called Inflow: Ready to Assign on Web)") the inflow can go to RTA or straight into a category.
- Consequence for savings: money moved to a tracking "savings" account is, to the plan, indistinguishable from money spent. The plan loses all knowledge of it; getting it back looks like new income. There is no intermediate state ("set aside, out of sight, but still mine and still purposed").
- "Savings" as an account type is purely cosmetic within the Cash group; YNAB has no account-level savings behaviour (no interest handling, no goal, no lock).

### Gaps
- No official statement was found describing which category to choose for a Tracking→Cash transfer, or whether choosing a normal spending category vs "Inflow: Ready to Assign" is preferred.
- CDs (certificates of deposit) are not mentioned by name in any current help article I read. The nearest guidance is the T-bill FAQ (see section 6).

## 2. What YNAB officially recommends for a savings account (on-budget vs tracking), the rule of thumb, and how it has moved

### Takeaway
The current official instruction is unambiguous for bank savings: "Set up your savings account as a Cash account (and choose the savings option). Assign those dollars to categories, and include your emergency savings, too." The only time-horizon rule of thumb in the current docs is that Cash accounts are for money you'll spend "in the near future (usually within the next year or two)"; Tracking is for "money you don't plan to spend soon, such as investments." But YNAB's blog simultaneously says you don't need a savings account at all, a 2022 YNAB blog tells retirees to put retirement accounts ON budget as "unlinked checking accounts", and the HSA article offers both options.

### Cited Findings
- Current help article (last updated 2026-07-10): "Set up your savings account as a Cash account (and choose the savings option). Assign those dollars to categories, and include your emergency savings, too. Follow the YNAB Method and make a plan for your spending." — [How to Assign Your Savings](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-assign-your-savings-rkJ_Yy3Rq)
- Same article, steps: "1. Add your savings account. 2. Add categories for those saving dollars. Vacation, bike, etc. 3. Assign all money in your savings account to those categories." and "That's it! Don't worry about spending your savings before you're ready." — [How to Assign Your Savings](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-assign-your-savings-rkJ_Yy3Rq)
- Same article on emergency funds: "We believe everyone should have an emergency fund. But should you assign those emergency fund dollars to your plan? **Yes, absolutely**!" — [How to Assign Your Savings](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-assign-your-savings-rkJ_Yy3Rq)
- Savings categories become spending categories: "Saved up and reached that goal? That **savings** category can then act as a **spending** category. Record the purchase(s) right into that category. If it was a one-time purchase, hide the category, or delete and set a new target to start saving up for the next one!" — [How to Assign Your Savings](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-assign-your-savings-rkJ_Yy3Rq)
- The URL `ynab.com/blog/your-budget-is-your-savings-category` (an older blog post) now 301-redirects to the help article above. — [redirect source](https://www.ynab.com/blog/your-budget-is-your-savings-category)
- Rule of thumb (time horizon): Cash = "spend money in the near future (usually within the next year or two)"; Tracking = "money you don't plan to spend soon, such as investments." — [Account Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq). The getting-started glossary (last updated 2026-11-06) gives the simpler split: "Money in **cash accounts** (like checking and savings) adds to what you have available to assign… **Tracking accounts** (like investment or retirement accounts) let you monitor balances without affecting your plan." — [Getting Started: YNAB Basics and Key Terms](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-ynab-basics-and-key-terms-BJCaIHaTZe)
- "You don't need a savings account at all" (YNAB blog, undated on page; text says "for the past 15 years at YNAB", implying c. 2019): "if you have a budget, you don't need a savings account at all. All your money can sit happily in your checking account while your budget keeps track of the jobs they have to do, whether that's saving for a new car, buying groceries this weekend, or just sitting around indefinitely in case you suddenly lose your job." and "for the past 15 years at YNAB, we've recommended folks keep one checking and one savings account. But we've always dreamed of the day when we could cut that down to just one account." and "aside from a few situations where a separate account is absolutely necessary (HSA's, retirement accounts, money market accounts, etc.), you really only need one account." — [Do I Need a Savings Account?](https://www.ynab.com/blog/do-you-even-need-a-savings-account)
- Jesse Mecham, Feb 25, 2019: "You can move money between your accounts—say, from your checking account to your savings account—without impacting your budget, at all." / "The transfer has no category in YNAB. We categorize income when new money arrives, but no new money has arrived—this money was already here." / "The real power in money management is focusing on what you want the money to do, not where it's located." / "So, theoretically, I could just have all my money in one account?" the answer is yes." — [What's the Difference Between Budget vs. Bank Account?](https://www.ynab.com/blog/the-relationship-between-your-budget-your-accounts-its-complicated)
- "How to Save Money" guide (still live, still uses pre-2024 terminology — "Rule One", "Rule Two", "Rule Four", "budget"): "So, does it matter where you keep it? You could keep all your money in your checking account. "Oh no! The horror! I'll spend it if I do that!" Actually, you won't." / "You can keep it in a savings account just as easily. But keep in mind: you don't need eleven savings accounts, just one will suffice." / "Folks, those are categories." / "It doesn't matter where you put savings dollars when you use a budget." — [How to Save Money guide](https://www.ynab.com/guide/how-to-save-money)
- Emergency fund location (same guide): "Keep your emergency fund money in any account you want, but since you hopefully won't use it too often, consider keeping these funds in a simple savings account. Remember: assign dollars to the emergency fund category in your budget." — [How to Save Money guide](https://www.ynab.com/guide/how-to-save-money)
- Counter-example to "investments/retirement = tracking": YNAB blog, Oct 20, 2022 (retirement drawdown): "One possible solution is to add your retirement accounts as unlinked checking accounts in YNAB. (Don't define these as tracking accounts if you want to incorporate this money into your budget.) - HSA/FSA: both options offered. "You actually don't need to add these accounts in YNAB at all." / "you have two options. - Sinking funds post (Jesse Mecham, published Apr 11, 2023, updated Oct 12, 2026), on where to keep the money: "You have two options, and the honest answer is that it depends on the amount. Separate savings accounts work, but they don't scale… Expense categories are simpler. - Ben Barlowe, May 15, 2025, casually acknowledges off-YNAB savings: "And yet you also have a sizable emergency fund sitting in a category or in an off-YNAB savings account." — [Emergency Fund vs. Month Ahead](https://www.ynab.com/blog/emergency-fund-vs-month-ahead)
- Two older YNAB blog posts specifically about tracking accounts (`/blog/what-is-the-point-of-a-tracking-account`, `/blog/the-how-and-why-of-tracking-accounts`) now both redirect to the Account Types help article, i.e. their content has been retired. — [redirect 1](https://www.ynab.com/blog/what-is-the-point-of-a-tracking-account); [redirect 2](https://www.ynab.com/blog/the-how-and-why-of-tracking-accounts)

### Inferences
- The consistent core is "savings accounts are on-budget (Cash) and every dollar in them is assigned to a category". What varies is everything around it: the horizon ("near future (usually within the next year or two)" is hedged twice — "usually", "or two"), whether a savings account is needed at all, whether long-horizon money (retirement, HSA) may be on-budget, and whether a "generic" savings/emergency pot is legitimate.
- The 1–2 year rule and the "all saving is delayed spending, even retirement" doctrine (section 4) pull in opposite directions: the doctrine says all future money deserves a job; the account model says money beyond ~2 years should leave the plan and become invisible to it. The Oct 2022 retirement post is YNAB itself working around that tension by mis-typing an investment account as checking.
- YNAB's answer to "I want savings separated from spending money" is categories/category groups + (since 2023) Focused Views, never account-level separation.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the pre-2024 help-centre wording for "Budget accounts vs Tracking accounts" (docs.youneedabudget.com / early support.ynab.com) because web.archive.org was unreachable, so I cannot prove first-hand whether the "(usually within the next year or two)" horizon changed over time (e.g. from "next year or so"). Anyone with Wayback access should diff snapshots of `support.ynab.com/en_us/account-types-an-overview-BkmGM0qCq` and the two retired tracking-account blog posts.
- The "Do I Need a Savings Account?" post shows no author/date in the rendered text I captured; the c. 2019 dating is inferred from "past 15 years at YNAB" (YNAB was founded in 2004).

## 3. Target (goal) types relevant to savings and their history, with month-to-month behaviour

### Takeaway
Three eras: (1) classic goals — Target Category Balance (TB), Target Category Balance by Date (TBD), Monthly Funding (MF); (2) Sept 2019–June 2020 — "Plan Your Spending"/"Needed for Spending" (NEED) added, MF renamed Monthly Contribution then Monthly Savings Builder, TB renamed Target Savings Balance / Savings Balance; (3) May 13, 2024 — "simple targets": the user picks a cadence (Weekly/Monthly/Yearly/Custom) and a plain-language behaviour ("Set aside another…", "Refill up to…", and for Custom also "Fill up to…" and "Have a balance of…"), with the old types still underneath (the API still reports TB/TBD/MF/NEED/DEBT). The only target that looks at the category's balance rather than at money assigned is "Have a balance of…", which cannot repeat and "is not meant to be spent from".

### Cited Findings
**Current behaviour (help articles last updated 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-05)**
- Purpose statement: "A target is added to a category to tell YNAB (and to remind you) of how much you want to assign to a category each month, even if you don't have the funds in your account yet." — [Understanding Target Types in YNAB](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC)
- Cadences: "Select how often you want the target to repeat (its cadence): _Weekly_, _Monthly_, _Yearly_, or _Custom_. If you don't want a target to repeat, choose the custom option." — [How to Use Targets](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-use-targets-rk5kkI9ks)
- The two core behaviours: "If you choose the "Set aside another..." behavior, the target will prompt you to assign the entire target amount again, even if funds from the previous month weren't fully spent and have rolled over. If you choose the "Refill up to..." behavior, the target will only ask you to replace what you spent or unassigned last period." — [How to Use Targets](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-use-targets-rk5kkI9ks)
- Monthly "Set aside another…": "the target will prompt you to assign the full amount of the monthly target again. With this option, money will build up in the category over time." Category examples given: "Rent, mortgage, auto maintenance, home repairs, medical expenses, investment account contributions, building up a savings category, or any other set expense that happens each month." — [Understanding Target Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC)
- Monthly "Refill up to…": "the target will prompt you to refill any funds that left the category in the previous month, up to that month's target amount. With this option, you'll only assign enough to fill the category up to the target amount." — [Understanding Target Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC)
- Onboarding summary (last updated 2026-10-29): "**Refill up to**: Prompts you assign enough new money to have the target amount available. Money that rolled over from last month helps satisfy the target. Best for spending categories where you have a maximum you want to spend in any given month." / "**Set aside another**: Prompts you to assign the target amount each period, even if money rolled over from last month. Best for non-monthly and savings categories and fixed expenses where money should build up over time." — [Getting Started: Targets in YNAB](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-targets-in-ynab-r1FTiSaTbe)
- Yearly "Set aside another…" and spending: "This works well when you want to save up for an expense, spend it, and then start saving again for next year. Once the due date month has passed, the target repeats automatically… Because "Set aside another" only tracks what you assign (not what you spend), spending from the category won't affect the new target period." Example: "Assign $600 for taxes by next year and the target will ask you for $50 each month." — [Understanding Target Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC)
- Period boundaries: "Since there can only be one target in a category in a single month, the new target period will start the month **after** the previous one ends." — [Understanding Target Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC)
- Custom targets: "Assign and spend up to this amount by a given date… Currently, custom targets can only be set to repeat monthly or yearly. For a one-time expense, turn repeat off. The target won't ask for money again after its due month, so there's no need to snooze or delete it." Custom behaviours: "**With the "Set aside..." behavior**, the target will prompt you to assign the full target amount by the due date… If the target repeats, you'll choose whether to "set aside another..." or "refill up to..." the target amount." / "**With the "Fill up to..." behavior,** the target will take into account any currently available funds in the category and prompt you to assign the remaining target amount by the due date." — [Understanding Target Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC)
- "Have a balance of…" (the savings-balance target): "**With the "Have a balance of..." behavior**, you can choose whether or not you'd like to set a due date. If you choose a due date, the target will prompt you to assign money so the Available balance of the category matches the target amount by the due date. This target does not repeat, and it's not meant to be spent from before it's due. It's best for large, one-time expenses." Example: "Set a target to have $10,000 set aside for a house down payment." Category examples: "house down payment, new car, new furniture, one-time medical expenses, computer replacement, or other large one-time purchases." — [Understanding Target Types](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC). Also: "This target is not meant to be spent from until the end of the target period, so if you'd like to spend as you go, choose the 'Set aside..." behavior instead. You have the option to repeat targets with a "Set aside..." behavior, but this isn't possible for targets with a "Have a balance of..." behavior." — [How to Use Targets](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-use-targets-rk5kkI9ks)
- Known wrinkles YNAB documents itself: (a) "Yearly and repeating custom targets track the money assigned across the whole target period, not just what's in the category now. If money was already assigned and spent during the current period, the target can show as met even though the category needs more." Workaround: "edit the target and change the "By" date… to the month you last spent from the category." (b) "Leftover funds don't count toward "Refill up to" targets until the new month begins." (c) Refunds: "They aren't counted towards a target, because the assumption is they were counted when the funds were originally assigned". (d) "Editing a target will change it in _all_ instances of that target, in prior and future months." (e) "There is no way to set biweekly targets in YNAB". (f) "If you're still using one of our former target types and choose to edit it, you may see the target disappear in previous months when it resets." — [How to Use Targets](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-use-targets-rk5kkI9ks)
- Rollover: YNAB rolls over "automagically" ("maintaining your accrued Available amounts, resetting your Assigned amounts, and adjusting for any overspending"). "Notice a target still asking you to assign the full amount, even though you have money left over from last month? That's expected for "Set aside another…" targets — they always prompt for the full amount." — [When the Month Rolls Over](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/when-the-month-rolls-over-a-guide-rkyyd6qC9)
- Snooze (launched Dec 4, 2023): "A snoozed target will no longer ask you to assign more money into the category, even if the target is not met… Target snooze only lasts for the month you snooze it in. When the next month begins, the target will ask for money again. You can only snooze a target in the current month". "Snoozed targets don't count towards the Underfunded amount in the Auto-Assign section". — [Snooze a Target](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/snooze-a-target-HyS4E5rZT); launch: [Now You Can Choose to Snooze](https://www.ynab.com/whats-new/now-you-can-choose-to-snooze)
- Debt-side targets exist only for paired categories: "YNAB has three special target types that can only be used when a category is paired with an account" — credit card "Pay Specific Amount Each Month", "Pay off Balance by Date", and "Monthly Debt Payment" for loans (amortization-based; "YNAB will also calculate how much interest is saved if you increase the monthly payment amount"). — [Targets for Categories Paired with Accounts](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/paired-targets-BJJI8rdC5)

### Inferences
- Mapping old→new (inferred from the API field semantics plus the docs; YNAB publishes no explicit mapping table): NEED + `goal_needs_whole_amount=true` = "Set aside another…"; NEED + `false` = "Refill up to…"; MF = legacy "Monthly Savings Builder" (assign X every month regardless), now reachable as Monthly + "Set aside another…"; TB / TBD = "Have a balance of…" without / with a date; DEBT = Monthly Debt Payment on loan-paired categories.
- Month-to-month behaviour when money is withdrawn from a savings category: "Set aside another…" ignores withdrawals entirely (asks for the same amount next period; it "only tracks what you assign (not what you spend)"); "Refill up to…" asks only to replace what left; "Have a balance of…" compares Available with the target, so a withdrawal re-opens the shortfall (per 2020 wording: "maintain the balance by replenishing any money spent"), but YNAB now says it is "not meant to be spent from" and "does not repeat". None of them distinguishes a withdrawal *for the goal's purpose* (buying the bike) from a raid (covering groceries) or from moving the money out to an investment account.
- There is no target type for an open-ended, never-spent, growing balance with a contribution rate AND a visible cumulative total goal (e.g. "add 500/month until I have 30,000, and keep it there"): monthly "Set aside another…" has no end amount; "Have a balance of…" has no fixed contribution and cannot repeat or auto-top-up on a cadence. Users must pick one or switch between them.
- A target is a prompt about *assigning*, never about real-world movement of money: nothing in targets knows whether the money is in a savings account, was transferred, or earned interest.

## 4. Method wording about saving: Four Rules → Five Questions → "What's it for?"; "there is no such thing as savings"

### Takeaway
YNAB's doctrine is that saving is not a distinct thing: "Saving, investing, giving—it's all spending" (method page, 2026); "There is no savings, only spending!" (Jesse Mecham Show, June 2025); "There is no such thing as saving money" (Ben Barlowe, Sept 2023). Savings must have a specific job. The method's public framing has been rewritten twice in two years — Four Rules (to late 2024), Five Questions (announced Dec 2024/Jan 2025), and by 2026 one question, "What's it for?", with five answers (Now, Later, Ease, You, Change) — and none of the three framings has a slot called "savings". Long-term wealth-building is reconciled only rhetorically ("retirement: the ultimate True Expense"; "a bill you pay that pays you back") and by sending the money off-plan.

### Inferences
- Yes, YNAB says savings needs a purpose/job, and says so emphatically; a purposeless "Savings" pot is described as the failure mode ("just "savings""; "spinning their wheels"). This is philosophy that directly explains product absence: if savings is not a category of thing, there is nothing to build a "savings feature" around.
- The doctrine is internally consistent for sinking funds and wishes, strained for emergency funds (a deliberately vague job — see section 7) and essentially silent for wealth-building: money invested for 20+ years gets a category only at the moment of contribution, then leaves the plan. "Give every dollar a job" stops applying to exactly the dollars with the longest horizon.
- The 2026 "For Ease" (month ahead) and "For Change" answers are in effect buffer/reserve savings, but are framed as something other than savings.
- Three framings in ~2 years, with the intermediate announcement deleted and older "Rule" language still live elsewhere, means a user reading YNAB material in 2026 meets at least three vocabularies for the same idea.

## 6. Interest income, investment growth and market-value adjustments: official guidance

### Takeaway
On-budget savings interest is income to Ready to Assign. For tracking (investment) accounts, gains, dividends, interest and employer contributions are recorded by reconciling to the new balance — a balance adjustment with no category, "informational only". YNAB "typically recommend[s]" not adding investment accounts at all and just using a contribution category.

### Cited Findings

- T-bills / short-term instruments: "Use Method One: create a category for the investment, record the outflow when you purchase it, and record the inflow once it matures." — [Tracking Investment Accounts](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/tracking-investment-accounts-a-guide-r1Bzjxd05)
- Payroll contributions: "that money never lands in your spending account, so there's nothing to plan for or record in YNAB. Just enter your take-home pay as income. If you're using Method Two, those contributions will show up in the account's balance — reconcile the tracking account to its new balance, and the adjustment will include your contributions along with any investment gains." — [Tracking Investment Accounts](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/tracking-investment-accounts-a-guide-r1Bzjxd05). Payroll article (last updated 2026-11-09): "**Payroll deductions, on the other hand, don't impact your day-to-day decision-making.** … there's no spending decision to make—and no built-in mechanism in YNAB to include them in your income transactions." Optional tracking: "Set up some Scheduled Transactions as repeating inflows in that account to track your contributions" and "Reconcile the account to update your account balance as it changes due to market fluctuation". — [Payroll Deductions](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/payroll-deductions-an-overview-HJMG_8WR9)
- Home value as an asset (Lea Velocci, Jul 17, 2025): create an unlinked account, "choose 'Asset (e.g. Investment)' as your account type"; update by "a balance adjustment. If the value went up, it's a positive adjustment; if it went down, it's negative. And since it's a tracking account there's no category needed here." — [Tracking Our Home Value](https://www.ynab.com/blog/tracking-our-home-value)
- On-budget invested accounts are discouraged because of fluctuation: "The checking account option is not recommended for invested accounts where the balance fluctuates." — [HSA article](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-add-a-health-savings-account-in-ynab-ByRXzZOAc); but the 2022 retirement post accepts it: "that asset goes up and down in value—so you won't have a perfectly accurate balance, but precision isn't necessary". — [Managing Your Retirement Budget](https://www.ynab.com/blog/managing-your-retirement-budget)

### Inferences
- In a tracking account, a reconciliation adjustment lumps together contributions made outside the plan, interest, dividends and market movement; YNAB keeps no record of return vs contribution. In Method One, a T-bill's matured inflow (principal + interest) arrives as an undifferentiated inflow that the user must categorize.
- Because Cash→Tracking contributions count as expenses and tracking gains count as nothing, YNAB's Income v Expense systematically shows investing as spending and investment income as invisible ("Net income in Income v Expense won't always match the change in your net worth, because it leaves out tracking account activity, starting balances, and credit card payments." — [Income v Expense](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/income-v-expense-Byu1BYWRq)).
- On-budget fluctuation has no sanctioned handling: if an on-budget account loses value, the adjustment must be categorized (it removes money from RTA or a category). YNAB's docs don't describe this case; they steer such accounts to tracking.

### Gaps
- No official guidance found for: CDs specifically; negative-return adjustments on on-budget accounts; multi-currency savings; how to treat interest on a savings account whose categories are meant to "keep" their own interest.

## 7. Evidence that the guidance is "personal preference", contradictory, or a soft spot (YNAB staff/first-party voices)

### Takeaway
First-party YNAB content openly contradicts itself on emergency funds ("We believe everyone should have an emergency fund" vs the founder's "Delete Your Emergency Fund") and repeatedly falls back on "it's up to you / it depends / there is no right or wrong answer" for where savings live, how much to keep and whether to track investments. I found hedging in abundance but no explicit YNAB admission that savings is a product weak spot.

### Inferences
- The contradiction pattern is structural, not sloppy: "every dollar needs a specific job" (doctrine) vs "people want a general cushion and long-term wealth" (reality). YNAB resolves each instance ad hoc — emergency fund as a True Expense (guide), as optional (2018), as counter to the core principle (2025), as mandatory (help centre 2026).
- "It's your money / up to you" is a brand value for YNAB (anti-prescriptive since the Dec 2024 shift away from "rules"), so hedging on savings is consistent with their stated philosophy; the cost is that new users get no single default for the most common setup questions (HYSA on or off budget? one pot or many? how do I see what I've actually saved?).
- A design that gives savings an explicit, first-class place while still attaching purposes would be addressing something YNAB has chosen not to model, not something YNAB tried and abandoned (no evidence of the latter was found).

### Gaps
- No support-agent replies were captured: YNAB's old community forum is gone, Reddit/mirrors were blocked, and searches surfaced only community (non-staff) answers. Claims that support says "personal preference" in tickets/Reddit are therefore unverified here; the hedging evidence above is all from published YNAB blog/help/podcast content.
- Podcast evidence comes from episode descriptions in the official RSS feed, not transcripts; Jesse's spoken wording may differ.
- No YouTube content was reviewed (the Wish Farm video is referenced by the help centre but not transcribed).

## 8. What the API / data model knows about "savings"

### Takeaway
The API (OpenAPI v1.91.0) exposes `savings` only as one of thirteen account `type` enum values, with no associated behaviour; the plan-relevant fact is the boolean `on_budget`. Savings *intent* exists only as category goal fields (`goal_type` TB/TBD/MF/NEED/DEBT plus cadence/rollover modifiers). There is no interest rate, no account↔category link for assets, no "savings category" flag, no asset sub-typing, and no return/contribution distinction. Debt, by contrast, has rate/minimum-payment/escrow fields and typed debt transactions.

### Cited Findings
- `AccountType` enum: `checking, savings, cash, creditCard, lineOfCredit, otherAsset, otherLiability, mortgage, autoLoan, studentLoan, personalLoan, medicalDebt, otherDebt`. — [YNAB OpenAPI spec v1.91.0](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Account fields: `id, name, type, on_budget, closed, note, balance, cleared_balance, uncleared_balance, transfer_payee_id, direct_import_linked, direct_import_in_error, last_reconciled_at, debt_original_balance, debt_interest_rates, debt_minimum_payments, debt_escrow_amounts, deleted` (+ formatted variants). `on_budget`: "Whether this account is "on budget" or not". `debt_original_balance` is marked deprecated ("will always be null"). — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Accounts tag description: "an account is either "on budget", where its activity is categorized and planned, or a tracking account, where only its balance is tracked (see the on_budget flag)." — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Category fields: `id, category_group_id, category_group_name, name, hidden, original_category_group_id, note, budgeted, activity, balance, goal_type, goal_needs_whole_amount, goal_day, goal_cadence, goal_cadence_frequency, goal_creation_month, goal_target, goal_target_month, goal_target_date, goal_percentage_complete, goal_months_to_budget, goal_under_funded, goal_overall_funded, goal_overall_left, goal_snoozed_at, deleted`. — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- `goal_type`: "The type of goal, if the category has a goal (TB='Target Category Balance', TBD='Target Category Balance by Date', MF='Monthly Funding', NEED='Plan Your Spending')"; enum `TB, TBD, MF, NEED, DEBT, null`. — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- `goal_needs_whole_amount`: "Indicates the monthly rollover behavior for "NEED"-type goals. When "true", the goal will always ask for the target amount in the new month ("Set Aside"). When "false", previous month category funding is used ("Refill"). For other goal types, this field will be null." — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- `goal_cadence`: "Value in range 0-14… For values 0, 1, 2, and 13, the goal's due date repeats every goal_cadence * goal_cadence_frequency, where 0 = None, 1 = Monthly, 2 = Weekly, and 13 = Yearly… For values 3-12 and 14, goal_cadence_frequency is ignored and the goal's due date repeats every goal_cadence, where 3 = Every 2 Months, 4 = Every 3 Months, ..., 12 = Every 11 Months, and 14 = Every 2 Years." `goal_day`: weekday (0 = Sunday) for weekly, else day of month ("null = Last day of Month"). — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Progress fields: `goal_under_funded` — "The amount of funding still needed in the current month to stay on track towards completing the goal within the current goal period. This amount will generally correspond to the 'Underfunded' amount in the web and mobile clients except when viewing a category with a Needed for Spending Goal in a future month. The web and mobile clients will ignore any funding from a prior goal period when viewing category with a Needed for Spending Goal in a future month."; `goal_overall_funded` — "The total amount funded towards the goal within the current goal period."; `goal_overall_left`; `goal_percentage_complete`; `goal_months_to_budget` ("The number of months, including the current month, left in the current goal period."); `goal_snoozed_at` ("The date/time the goal was snoozed."). — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Write side (targets are now writable via the API): `goal_target` — "If value is specified and goal has not already been configured for category, a monthly goal will be created… If goal_type is not specified, it will default to 'NEED', or to 'MF' for Credit Card Payment categories and 'DEBT' for categories paired to a loan account." `goal_needs_whole_amount` — "When true, the goal is configured as 'Set aside another...'. When false, the goal is configured as 'Refill up to...'." `goal_frequency` enum `monthly, weekly, yearly` "configures a recurring 'NEED' target". Credit card 'TBD' is "(Pay Off Balance by Date)". — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Transfers: transactions carry `transfer_account_id`; "Splits are not allowed on tracking accounts or on transfers between on-budget accounts; a transfer to a tracking account can be a split." — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Debt-specific transaction typing exists: "If the transaction is a debt/loan account transaction, the type of transaction" with enum `payment, refund, fee, interest, escrow, balanceAdjustment, credit, charge`. No equivalent for asset/savings accounts. — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)
- Month-level: `to_be_budgeted` ("The available amount for 'Ready to Assign'"), `age_of_money` ("The Age of Money as of the month"). Money-movement endpoints exist (`/plans/{plan_id}/money_movements`, `…/money_movement_groups`) for category↔category/RTA moves. The API now says "plans" rather than "budgets" in paths. — [YNAB OpenAPI spec](https://api.ynab.com/papi/open_api_spec.yaml)

### Inferences
- What the model knows: (1) whether an account's money is in the plan (`on_budget`); (2) a cosmetic label `savings` among on-budget types; (3) per-category targets, including a balance-style target (TB/TBD) and assign-style targets (MF/NEED). What it does not know: which categories are "savings" vs "spending" (no flag — only inferable from goal type or naming); where a category's money physically sits; interest rates or yields on asset accounts; asset kinds (`otherAsset` covers investments, retirement, property); contribution vs growth; any link between a savings account and a goal; any lock/"do not spend" state.
- A tracking account in API terms is simply `on_budget: false` with type `otherAsset`/`otherLiability`; loans are separate typed accounts that are also off-budget but carry rate/payment metadata and can be paired with a category. Assets have no pairing concept.
- The persistence of TB/TBD/MF/NEED codes after the 2024 UI simplification confirms the "simple targets" release was a presentation layer over the 2019–2020 goal engine.
- Whether "Have a balance of…" maps to TB/TBD, and whether custom "Fill up to…" is NEED with `goal_needs_whole_amount=false`, is inferred, not stated in the spec.

### Gaps
- The spec gives no description for `DEBT` inside the `goal_type` description string (only in the enum), and none for `debt_interest_rates`/`debt_minimum_payments`/`debt_escrow_amounts` beyond their schema.
- I did not fetch the human-readable docs at api.ynab.com (the page renders client-side and returned empty); everything is from the OpenAPI YAML, which is the authoritative machine spec.
